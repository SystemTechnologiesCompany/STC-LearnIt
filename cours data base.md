# STC Learnit — course data base

Working research notebook for the subjects currently listed in `learn.js`. This is a source map and first-pass topic index, not a claim that every listed topic is the current semester syllabus. University offerings change; before publishing lessons, capture the exact academic year, degree, specialty, level/semester, official course title, and source document.

## How to use this file

- Add one course per row/section and preserve its official title (Arabic/French/English) alongside the Learnit display title.
- Prefer course descriptions, semester plans, lecture notes, and exam documents published by Kasdi Merbah University Ouargla (UKMO) or Algeria’s Ministry of Higher Education.
- Record a source date and retrieve date. Mark archived/old curricula as historical; do not present them as current without confirmation.
- For quizzes, write original questions from verified course material and track the source page/page number. Avoid copying large chunks of course notes.

### Practical ideas for Learnit lesson design

1. Start with a concrete observation or Algerian example, then introduce the vocabulary and mechanism (e.g., compare two rock textures before explaining formation).
2. Use a repeatable pattern: short explanation → visual/specimen or map → guided classification/interpretation → a few retrieval questions → recap.
3. Make geology questions ask students to interpret evidence: classify a mineral from properties, order strata, compare magnitude/intensity, read a graph/map, or justify a resource/environment conclusion. Do not reuse OpenLearn quiz questions verbatim.
4. Use progressive scaffolding: identify/describe first, explain processes next, then interpret unfamiliar data or combine concepts in a unit/final exam.
5. Localize case studies and maps to Algeria/Sahara and UKMO’s official syllabus; OpenLearn’s British examples are teaching examples, not Algerian geology facts.

## Level and assessment blueprint for every Learnit subject

The app’s planned structure is **5 units × 20 levels = 100 levels per subject**. This blueprint sets a consistent quality bar while keeping topics distinct between all 30 subjects. It is a build framework, not permission to fill levels with generic or repeated material.

### Five unit roles

1. **Foundations:** vocabulary, prerequisite ideas, instruments/notations, and basic classification.
2. **Core concepts:** the discipline’s main models, rules, systems, or processes.
3. **Methods and applications:** calculations, lab/field methods, case analysis, interpretation, or practical tasks appropriate to the subject.
4. **Integration:** connect multiple modules; explain unfamiliar cases/data; compare approaches and diagnose common errors.
5. **Mastery and transfer:** cumulative applied problems, subject-specific projects/cases, exam readiness, and final assessment.

For each specialty, the detailed 20-level list must come from actual module learning outcomes and chapter plans. A unit can cover one coherent module block and its prerequisites; avoid repeating the exact same chapter in multiple units. In languages, progression should build communicative and textual competence; in law, cases and jurisdiction-specific texts; in medicine, carefully scoped course concepts without clinical advice; in engineering/sciences, worked calculations and laboratory interpretation; in humanities/social sciences, source analysis, argument, and methods; in STAPS, movement/training concepts and safe application.

### Required record for every level

Every level should have its own unique record with: `subject`, `faculty`, `degree/specialty`, `year/semester`, `unit`, `level number`, `official chapter/module`, `2–4 observable learning outcomes`, prerequisite links, original concise lesson, one worked example or practice activity, quiz items with answer explanations, source URL/file and page/section, and review status. Maintain a question bank keyed to its source and learning outcome; check question text and correct answer against all earlier levels in that subject before publishing, so repeated questions are caught and deliberate cumulative review is labeled as review.

### Difficulty and progression checks

- Levels 1–4: recognize, define, label, and explain one idea at a time.
- Levels 5–8: compare, classify, perform standard procedures, and solve guided problems.
- Levels 9–12: apply methods with less scaffolding and interpret simple data/cases.
- Levels 13–16: connect multiple concepts, diagnose errors, and defend a conclusion.
- Levels 17–19: solve unfamiliar or multi-step problems under exam-like conditions.
- Level 20: unit exam covering the unit’s outcomes, with a balanced mix of recall, application, interpretation, and explanation; passing unlocks the next unit.

Vary these cognitive tasks by discipline rather than forcing every subject into numeric exercises. Set pass thresholds only after a course owner approves the exam rules and accessibility needs. Before activating a subject, require a source audit, duplicate-question scan, answer-key review, faculty/degree check, and coverage check confirming every intended module outcome is assessed at least once.

### Use and quality rules for LibreTexts materials
